## What sk-13a delivers

**The solver** walks the constraints once, in order, merges included (now
equations). It gives each one exactly one class:
- **Conflict**, if it has a degree-0 refutation with what was kept before
  it, shown with an irreducible support;
- otherwise its derivable equations are dropped, and it is **Over** if the
  rest is structurally over-determined (by what the equations depend on);
- otherwise **Kept**, or **Amber** when everything about it is implied.

**What is proven:**
- the answer is honest;
- honest classifications are unique, so no loophole is left;
- the semantic meanings follow;
- the existing laws hold over the solved system.

### S1. Polynomials as vectors, and the bridge
- **Rows** are integer polynomials in normal form (`lib/zring.bend`'s
  ZTerm lists), with a combination (equation, integer coefficient).
- **Invariant:** in every ordered field, a row's value is Σ c·den·g.
- **Normal forms proven reduced:** sorted, distinct monomials, no zero
  coefficients.
- **The non-root lemma:** a nonzero integer polynomial has an integer
  point where it is nonzero, by induction on variables or by Kronecker
  substitution. I'll pick after a short prototype.
- **Consequences:**
  - `Ident` holds iff the normal forms are equal;
  - transfer to every ordered field;
  - identities read coefficient by coefficient;
  - `Depends(e, v)` holds iff v appears in the normal form of e (the
    lemma applied to the derivative), so the solver's adjacency from
    normal forms is the spec's dependence (Q30).

### S2. Degree-0 elimination over all equations
- Each equation is normalized; its variables (from the normal form) give
  the structure's adjacency.
- Fraction-free Gaussian elimination over monomial vectors, constraint by
  constraint, against the earlier solved rows and c's own earlier
  non-dropped equations.
- Outcomes: residual 0, dropped with its combination; a nonzero
  constant, a refutation candidate; otherwise tentatively solved.
- Echelon form: distinct leading monomials other than 1.
- Proves `DropsOk` and the independence facts.

### S3. Exact simplex with Bland's rule, and the deletion filter
- **The system:** monomials are the unknowns. Equalities come from the
  earlier solved rows plus c's tentative ones; inequalities from the kept
  sides, the entity sides and c's sides. Strictness uses the standard
  extra variable.
- **Output:** a linearized feasible point, or a Motzkin certificate.
- **Chinneck's deletion filter for every conflict.** It tests each
  candidate support with **all equations of its members (dropped ones
  included)**, their sides, c, and the entity sides (audit 4.1).
- **Proves:**
  - termination (Bland);
  - certificate soundness: `ConflictOk`'s refutation, and amber sides
    (`Implied`, with the `Ref`s recorded for the verdict);
  - "a feasible linearized point means no refutation": `NoRefute`,
    `NotImplied`, and the irreducibility in `ConflictOk`.
- Check in with Danielle after S3.
