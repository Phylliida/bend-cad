## What sk-13a delivers

**The solver** walks the constraints once, in order, merges included (now
equations). It gives each one exactly one class:
- **Conflict**, if it has a degree-0 refutation with what was kept before
  it, shown with an irreducible support;
- otherwise its derivable equations are dropped, and it is **Over** if the
  rest is structurally over-determined (by what the equations depend on);
- otherwise **Kept**, or **Amber** when everything about it is implied.

**What is proven:**
- the answer is honest;
- honest classifications are unique, so no loophole is left;
- the semantic meanings follow;
- the existing laws hold over the solved system.

**For your sketches:**
- Repeats, including repeated OnCircles, become amber and certify at the
  first precision. Today oncircle-pairs with its repeats is refused after
  88 s.
- Conflicting dimensions, Radius −1, a distance of −5, an angle against
  its opposite, and conflicting merges become red with irreducible
  supports.
- vertical-twice stays refused until sk-13b.

### S1. Polynomials as vectors, and the bridge
*Done 2026-10-19 (the mathematics; rows move to S2).* As built, gated
through `tests/ident_laws.bend`:
- `lib/redpoly.bend`: `norm_red`, every normal form reduced (terms
  ascending strictly by monomial, no zero coefficient, sorted monomials);
  the monomial order is reflexive, flips and is transitive.
- `lib/nonroot.bend`: the non-root lemma, by induction on variables (the
  Kronecker route needs base-B digit uniqueness and a dominance bound,
  both heavier, so I didn't prototype it). Split p = A + x_h B at the
  smallest variable: if A is not 0, x_h = 2a makes p = a(1 + 2B), odd
  times nonzero; otherwise p = x_h B, with every coordinate of the
  witness kept nonzero. No inequalities needed.
- `lib/identpf.bend`: `Ident(e1, e2)` holds exactly when the cleared
  e1 - e2 normalizes to 0 (`ident_ok`, `qzero_ident`); identities hold in
  every ordered field (`ident_all`); the same against 0.
- **Dependence, as built:** `dep(e, v)` normalizes the derivative of e in
  v, and `dep_ok`, `nodep_ok` and `dep_of` show it decides `Depends`
  exactly (x - x + y: y only). This differs from the bullet below, which
  reads v off e's own normal form. Agreement of the two would need a
  derivative on normal forms commuting with the symbolic one. That isn't
  needed for correctness, and the cost is one small normalization per
  (equation, variable it mentions).
- `lib/coefpf.bend`: `coef(e, m)`, the rational coefficient of a
  monomial. Linear over sums, differences, negation, constant multiples
  and `lin` (`coef_lin`, exact equalities); `ident_coef` gives an identity
  equal coefficients at every monomial. This bridges `Ident` to linear
  equations over monomials for S2 and S3.
- Moved to S2: rows with their combinations and value invariant (their
  shape follows the elimination), and "a variable e doesn't mention has
  derivative 0" for the adjacency.

- **Rows** are integer polynomials in normal form (`lib/zring.bend`'s
  ZTerm lists), with a combination (equation, integer coefficient).
- **Invariant:** in every ordered field, a row's value is Σ c·den·g.
- **Normal forms proven reduced:** sorted, distinct monomials, no zero
  coefficients.
- **The non-root lemma:** a nonzero integer polynomial has an integer
  point where it is nonzero, by induction on variables or by Kronecker
  substitution. I'll pick after a short prototype.
- **Consequences:**
  - `Ident` holds iff the normal forms are equal;
  - transfer to every ordered field;
  - identities read coefficient by coefficient;
  - `Depends(e, v)` holds iff v appears in the normal form of e (the
    lemma applied to the derivative), so the solver's adjacency from
    normal forms is the spec's dependence (Q30).

### S2. Degree-0 elimination over all equations
*Done 2026-10-19 at the basis level; the per-constraint walk and
`DropsOk` move to S4.* As built, gated through `tests/elim_laws.bend`
and `tests/ident_laws.bend`:
- `lib/deppf.bend`: the adjacency `dadj(e)` keeps the variables e
  mentions that it depends on (`dep`). Each is depended on; e depends on
  nothing outside it (an unmentioned variable has derivative 0 in every
  field).
- `lib/elim.bend` (the code):
  - Rows are integer polynomials in normal form, oldest first. Row j is
    aligned with basis entry j and records its combination: bt times its
    own equation plus cb (one integer per earlier entry), with bt > 0.
  - Reduction is fraction-free, r := |b| r - sgn(b) a R. The pivot is
    the residual's largest monomial, 1 only for a nonzero constant.
  - `proto/probe_elim`: a few ms per regression sketch, coefficients
    under 90 bits.
- `lib/elimpf.bend`: the invariants.
  - A row's polynomial and its recorded combination have the same
    coefficients.
  - The rows are echelon: each is nonzero at its pivot and every later
    row is 0 there.
  - The residual is reduced, 0 at every pivot, and its combination is as
    long as the basis.
  - A nonzero residual extends the rows and the basis (`new_rinv`).
- `lib/drvpf.bend`: a zero residual is a drop, with multipliers
  -cb_k den(B_k) / (bt den(e)) (`derivable`, the spec's `Ident`).
- `lib/indpf.bend`: a nonzero residual is independent of the basis
  (`indep`, the spec's NotDerivable), by a dual certificate.
  Back-substituted weights give a functional that kills every row, hence
  every basis equation, but not e.
- **Moved to S4:** walking a constraint's equations (each dropped, or a
  new row and basis entry) and `DropsOk` itself. Both need the pass's
  basis at c to be `presolved(sk, red, ds, c)`, which is the pass's own
  invariant; `own_rest` is that walk's accumulation.
- Pivot 1 (a constant residual) is allowed as a row. It marks a
  refutation candidate for S3 and keeps `DropsOk` exact for the
  equations after it.

- Each equation is normalized; its variables (from the normal form) give
  the structure's adjacency.
- Fraction-free Gaussian elimination over monomial vectors, constraint by
  constraint, against the earlier solved rows and c's own earlier
  non-dropped equations.
- Outcomes: residual 0, dropped with its combination; a nonzero
  constant, a refutation candidate; otherwise tentatively solved.
- Echelon form: distinct leading monomials other than 1.
- Proves `DropsOk` and the independence facts.

### S3. Exact simplex with Bland's rule, and the deletion filter
- **The system:** monomials are the unknowns. Equalities come from the
  earlier solved rows plus c's tentative ones; inequalities from the kept
  sides, the entity sides and c's sides. Strictness uses the standard
  extra variable.
- **Output:** a linearized feasible point, or a Motzkin certificate.
- **Chinneck's deletion filter for every conflict.** It tests each
  candidate support with **all equations of its members (dropped ones
  included)**, their sides, c, and the entity sides (audit 4.1).
- **Proves:**
  - termination (Bland);
  - certificate soundness: `ConflictOk`'s refutation, and amber sides
    (`Implied`, with the `Ref`s recorded for the verdict);
  - "a feasible linearized point means no refutation": `NoRefute`,
    `NotImplied`, and the irreducibility in `ConflictOk`.
