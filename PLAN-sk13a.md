## What sk-13a delivers

**The solver** walks the constraints once, in order, merges included (now
equations). For each one it finds:
- equations that are constant combinations of earlier solved ones
  (dropped);
- a degree-0 refutation against what was kept before it (red, Conflict,
  with an irreducible support);
- redundancy (amber).

Only then does it use structure (red, Over, with a backward-looking
witness).

**What is proven:**
- the answer is honest (`Honest`);
- honest classifications are unique (`honest_unique`), so no loophole is
  left in what is red, amber or dropped;
- the semantic meanings, as corollaries;
- the existing laws, restated over the solved system.

**For your sketches:**
- Repeats, including repeated OnCircles, become amber and certify at the
  first precision. Today oncircle-pairs with its repeats is refused after
  88 s.
- Conflicting dimensions, Radius −1, a distance of −5, an angle against
  its opposite, and conflicting merges ("conflicts with Horizontal #2")
  become red with irreducible supports.
- vertical-twice stays refused until sk-13b: its dependency needs
  multipliers of degree above 0.

### S1. Polynomials as vectors, and the bridge
- **Rows.** Integer polynomials in normal form (`lib/zring.bend`'s ZTerm
  lists) with a combination: a list of (equation, integer coefficient).
- **Invariant.** A row's value is Σ c·den·g in every ordered field
  (`lib/fring.bend`, `lib/qnorm.bend`).
- **Normal forms proven reduced** (audit 3.8): sorted, distinct
  monomials, no zero coefficients. Today only their soundness is proven.
- **The non-root lemma:** a nonzero integer polynomial has an integer
  point where it is nonzero. Two routes; I'll pick the easier one in Bend
  after a short prototype:
  - induction on variables, using the root count of a univariate
    polynomial;
  - Kronecker substitution, where each monomial becomes a distinct power
    of M and the largest term dominates.
- **Consequences:** `Ident` iff equal normal forms; transfer to every
  ordered field; identities read coefficient by coefficient (so a
  linearized feasible point excludes every refutation).

### S2. Degree-0 elimination over all equations
- Fraction-free Gaussian elimination over monomial vectors, constraint by
  constraint, against the solved rows kept so far *and c's own earlier
  equations*.
- Outcomes: residual 0, dropped with its combination; a nonzero constant,
  a refutation candidate; otherwise tentatively solved.
- Pivots are distinct leading monomials other than 1 (echelon form).
- Proves the `Drop0` and `Deg0` parts of law 5.

### S3. Exact simplex with Bland's rule, and the deletion filter
- **The system.** Monomials are the unknowns. Equalities come from the
  solved rows plus c's tentative ones; inequalities from kept sides,
  entity sides and c's sides. Strictness uses the standard extra variable.
- **Output:** a linearized feasible point, or a Motzkin certificate.
- **Chinneck's deletion filter for every conflict** (audit 3.2): S2's
  candidates and the simplex's alike. Each member is removed in turn with
  a simplex run, and members whose removal leaves a refutation are
  dropped from the support. Irreducibility follows because feasibility
  only grows as rows are removed.
- **Proves:**
  - termination (Bland);
  - certificate soundness, giving `Conflict0` and `Amber0` (the premises
    plus the side's negation);
  - "a feasible linearized point means no refutation", giving `Mz0`,
    `Over0`(b) and `ConflictMin`.
- Check in with Danielle after S3.

## Risks

- **The non-root lemma** (S1) and **reduced normal forms**: classical, but
  real proofs. They are the bridge every later phase uses.
- **Bland's rule termination** (S3): a classical combinatorial argument,
  moderately hard to formalize. If it's far harder than expected, I'll
  stop and ask before considering alternatives.
- **`solve_regular`** (S5): from the plan's contraction bound to an
  explicit two-sided inverse (diagonal dominance, then a block-triangular
  inverse) is new proof work.
- **`honest_unique`** (S6): a new kind of proof (two answers compared by
  induction). Its lemmas are small, but the induction carries a lot.
- **Re-proving `solve_complete`** (S7): C1–C6 were long. The change
  should be a substitution, but the proof is large.
- **Merges as equations** (S0a): larger blocks until the presolve; record
  the regression timings.
- **Check times:** small import closures; the gate checks each test's
  closure.
- **Scope:** sk-13a is a large milestone. Commit at each step with the
  gate green, and check in after S0 and after S3.
